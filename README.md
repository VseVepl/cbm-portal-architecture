## Architecture-wide design doctrine

Every architecture and product decision must consider scalability, configurability, extensibility, customization, controlled override capability, and explicit applicability to the correct business contexts. Business-specific variation should be supported within one governed platform wherever appropriate.

Business and Tenant Data Isolation and the Business Structure Hierarchy and Structural Integrity are strict, non-configurable Stable Core constraints. The currently finalized Customer Business Structure is Organization → Distributor / Shop; Distributor and Shop are terminal structural entities. The former two-category Shop classification is discontinued. No customization, configuration, delegation, extension, or override may weaken or bypass these structural constraints. Flexibility must always remain within Main Portal governance and all other Stable Core rules. Ownership Links, Owner IDs, and Ownership IDs must remain distinct from Business Structure and Business Relationships. Business Identity comprises two distinct dimensions: Legal Identity and Professional Identity. One Legal Identity may be associated with multiple distinct Professional Identities, such as separately named and located Shops operating under one taxpayer identity; this does not merge their operational identities or create separate legal persons. This model applies to Main Portal, Sub-Portals, Organizations, Distributors, Shops, and eligible operational units. Business Available Authority Scope must also remain distinct from explicit User Assigned Authority Scope and action Permissions. Within an Organization, multiple Distributor entities may be grouped as a Distribution Network, and multiple Shop entities may be grouped as a Retail Network; Hybrid Network denotes an Organization operating both distribution and retail operations. Every Distributor and Shop must have exactly one direct Parent Organization when created; neither may be unassigned, standalone, or have multiple structural parents. This Parent Organization relationship establishes structural membership only and does not determine ownership or identity. A Distributor–Shop Mapping does not require either party to select, know, or disclose the other party's Parent Organization. Every Distributor and Shop must have a Professional Identity; its own Legal Identity is optional and must be explicitly established where applicable. Ownership is independent of structural placement. These are operating configurations/groupings, not separate Business Identities or ownership relationships. Mapping is typed and purpose-specific: Distributor–Shop Mapping is only one Mapping type, not a universal boundary. It requires two-way verification using a Shop-generated Secret Code followed by a separate Distributor-generated confirmation code. Both codes must expire, be single-use, be rate-limited, and support revocation. Active Mapping permits only Mapping-Type-authorized business transactions and necessary information exchange; it does not change ownership, structural membership, Parent Organization, or either party's data boundary, and does not grant broad data access. User Scope Mapping explicitly determines which entities or locations a User may access. GST Registration Assignment may target any CBM entity/context enabled by Main Portal, subject to verified taxpayer identity and legal registration coverage. Domain History is domain-specific and retained for the relevant business lifetime; Audit Trail is comprehensive, separately permissioned, and governed by its own retention policy.

## Documentation rule

Only decisions explicitly finalized by the project owner belong in the authoritative master document. Discussion notes, rejected approaches, unapproved recommendations, and unresolved matters must not be presented as finalized decisions. Keep discussion history separate from the master source of truth.
